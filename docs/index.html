Is the store brand of canned tuna priced too low?

A demand model estimated on 93 Chicago supermarkets says yes: shoppers of the store brand barely react to its price, and the few who leave move to brands with a better margin. Move the prices below to see the trade-off.

How to read it

  • Raising only the store brand from $0.75 towards $0.85–0.94 lifts category profit in the model, with the whole 90% interval above zero.
  • The best price sits at the top of the range ever charged, so the honest recommendation is a store-group test, not a chain-wide change.
  • Raising all four brands looks far more profitable here, but that is what the model cannot judge: see the limits.

Limits

  • Price variation comes mostly from temporary promotions; shoppers stock up on deals, which a permanent price change does not trigger.
  • The model does not see shoppers switching store, or the traffic a cheap staple brings in.
  • Costs come from the dataset's margin field, based on average inventory cost. Data are from the 1990s.
Method, code and full results: github.com/francisco-carcano/retail-price-elasticity. Data: Dominick's Finer Foods, courtesy of the James M. Kilts Center for Marketing, University of Chicago Booth School of Business.
